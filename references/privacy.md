# Required patient-privacy warning and review

For a separately requested private execution/run audit, also follow [audit-export privacy](audit-exports.md). Mail-result omission alone does not remove identifiers embedded in wrapper code, copied scripts or timing files.

Before requesting a teaching, abstract or presentation upload, say briefly: “Please upload a de-identified version only; these folders feed a public website. I will flag possible patient identifiers before saving or linking the file.” Reuse a warning already given in the current upload flow; do not repeat it unnecessarily. Explain that receiving a file in chat has already uploaded it to that chat environment; checking afterward cannot undo that transfer. Do not claim this account, chat, plugin or private staging is HIPAA compliant or approved by the user's institution.

## Initial screening, private preparation and final review

The initial source screen is limited to surfaces the host can actually inspect; do not call it complete visual review when a deck cannot yet be rendered. Already flagged material must not be copied or converted through this ordinary staging pathway. For initially not-flagged PowerPoint, full rendered-page review may require conversion first. Tell the user explicitly that this creates private original/PDF copies before final review and obtain permission for that preparation; the helper requires the matching source-screening and private-copy assertions. Permission to prepare privately is not permission to publish, nor institutional clearance or proof of de-identification. If ordinary private staging is unsuitable or permission is declined, request a de-identified PDF exported locally with approved tools.

After preparation, review all converted pages visually and inspect the required source/hidden surfaces as available before public save/link approval. If identifiers are discovered then, pause, report the retained private folder without quoting identifiers, and ask before deleting any existing copy. Do not make additional flagged copies. The helper's pending-review label and save flags are review assertions, not PHI screening. Failed preparation may also leave original/partial files; inspect its reported location and minimal receipts before any retry or cleanup.

If possible patient-identifying information is detected, explicitly warn the user immediately, before routine work continues. Do not leave the finding in an internal checklist or merely ask whether publication is intended. Say for example: “Possible patient-identifying information appears on slide 7 (a chart screenshot with a patient label). I have paused saving/linking this file. Please provide a de-identified replacement.” Name the page/slide and identifier category without quoting identifiers, reproducing the image or putting PHI in logs, proposal diffs, filenames or pending notes. Preserve the underlying academic record independently, with its optional attachment null or existing link unchanged.

Review source text, rendered slides/pages and images (OCR where available), screenshots, headers/footers, captions, filenames, PDF metadata/annotations/attachments, and PPT notes/comments/hidden slides/embedded objects when accessible. Check the final PDF independently after conversion. Text search alone misses burned-in labels; review all pages visually. If a surface cannot be inspected, disclose the gap and hold public placement until a reviewed replacement or institutional privacy clearance resolves it. Screening can miss identifiers and is not a certificate of de-identification. A no-findings result means only that the inspected surfaces revealed no obvious identifiers.

Look for patient names/initials, medical record/account numbers, contact details, identifying geography, patient-related dates beyond year, ages over 89, faces, barcodes/QR codes and distinctive case details that could identify someone in combination. Distinguish patient identifiers from legitimate presenter/author names and the academic event date; do not label every date/name as PHI. HHS recognizes Safe Harbor and Expert Determination; Safe Harbor requires removing the specified identifiers and no actual knowledge that remaining information identifies the individual. See [HHS de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html). Do not claim removal of names alone satisfies it.

Recommend a fresh de-identified copy prepared locally with institution-approved tools: remove identifying text and images or use real redaction, remove hidden content and metadata, export a fresh PDF, then inspect both visible pages and extractable content. A black rectangle, cropped view or PDF conversion alone is not reliable redaction. Keep identifiable originals only in institution-approved storage; the public repository and its ignored raw folders are not suitable PHI storage. Do not create additional copies of a flagged file in ordinary unencrypted private staging. If a copy already exists, explain where it was saved without reproducing PHI and obtain authorization for any deletion; do not promise to erase chat uploads or remote copies. Use the platform's available removal controls and institutional privacy/security process if an unintended disclosure may have occurred.

Do not silently redact, automatically upload to an outside OCR/conversion service or treat a general “go ahead” as resolution. If the user wants to retain identifiable material, route them to their institutional privacy officer for the appropriate approved handling/authorization; this plugin's public-site attachment pathway remains paused for unresolved identifiers. Clear the warning only after inspecting the replacement or verifying relevant institutional clearance. Show public-use approval separately from privacy review. Store only a minimal review receipt with inspected surfaces, limitations, outcome, hash and time outside the public repository; never the detected PHI. The save helper's flags assert that these reviews actually happened; they do not perform detection or legal certification.
