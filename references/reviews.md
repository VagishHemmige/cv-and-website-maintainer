# Routine and broader reviews

Use questions.md's category-first question: invite the actual item/change in the first answer, with no-change and explicit revisit choices where supported. Reuse incoming corrections and continue the existing eligible queue after dispositions. Missing facts may still need one follow-up; factual answers never bypass exact proposal approval or deferred conditions.

For actual routine no-change answers, follow [confirmation batching](confirmation-batching.md): stage each answer durably in an authorized private writable run directory, then flush together at natural breaks. Keep deferrals and approved changes on their immediate safeguarded paths. Read saved confirmation history before repeating questions; batches do not complete the broader review.

For deferred questions, follow [structured deferrals](deferrals.md). Read questions_to_ask and revisit metadata from the helper, not every preserved original question. Honor chosen dates/exact named triggers, record one actual offer and suppress later-cycle nudges until a real user response/rescheduling. A broader sweep or repeated candidate discovery does not override a deferral. Honor known legacy prose deferrals while a separately approved migration is pending; never silently pick a date or rewrite their history.

Load preferences.md and private history first. This is a host-executed workflow, not a bundled scheduler or storage service. Only a successfully configured automation makes reviews automatic. Never mutate records in an unattended run; prepare candidates for approval.

For routine reviews, discover available authorized email/calendar source tools anew and restrict queries to confirmed selections. Use discovery.md for evidence and report coverage honestly. Cross-check actual YAML and consumer links, known mentees, projects, abstracts and bibliography keys. Resolve existing references and identify plausible missing associations from explicit evidence, not just syntax errors. Coauthorship or attendance alone establishes a candidate, not mentoring or project membership. No schema expansion is implicit.
Compare current evidence with dated confirmations and dispositions. Report only new/actionable findings as findings; a user-requested regular submission reminder is separate and may still be delivered when no findings appear. Retain unresolved items without re-asking on every run. Record successful searches separately by source; failed sources do not advance watermarks. An unavailable source is not an empty search result.

For the broader review, display each selected category's full current list and ask which items changed, with a no-changes choice. Categories may include active projects, active mentees, appointments/service, grants, credentials/memberships, pending events/attachments and public descriptions. One category selection is one question; follow up on selected items one at a time. Use a checklist only if supported by the host; otherwise accept item names/numbers or no changes in chat. Do not assume a single-select question tool supports checkboxes.
Track broader review due dates independently of successful discovery. A skipped/failed routine run must not delay the broader review indefinitely. When aligned to every N weekly cycles, compute due dates from the configured anchor, and record completion only after the user reviews or explicitly defers. If a broader review remains unanswered, retain one pending review rather than create duplicates.

A project is a body of work the user finds useful to track together. Reuse known projects and aliases, propose clusters privately, and let boundaries develop rather than block setup. Outputs can conceptually belong to multiple projects or none; inspect schema/consumers before representing relationships. Combining/splitting projects requires concrete proposals and compatible consumers.
CVs often omit projects and mentees. Authorized correspondence, grants, repositories and role-labelled rosters can support candidate discovery. Explicit supervision is stronger evidence than repeated contact, coauthorship or calendar attendance. Confirm mentee/collaborator/neither before public changes. Report completeness against known records, not a claim to identify everyone.
Project inactivity alone does not establish closure. Grant endings, archived repositories, final reports or publications may prompt a status question but do not prove the broader project ended. Distinguish completed, paused and abandoned in the question; map only to supported schema values after confirmation. Preserve unchanged status without evidence. Reuse answers and revisit dates.

Before record writes, route to the exact-proposal workflow. Do not copy correspondence or sensitive details into public YAML. Save review history privately and give the owner the output handoff after approved changes.

Before dispatch follow [scheduling](scheduling.md) and load the canonical settings from [preferences](preferences.md). The complete conditional setup inventory is [questionnaire](questionnaire.md). Do not start a broader-cycle counter without a configured anchor. Cross-check forward references, derived reverse views, prose/output mismatches, project membership/output candidates, resources, duplicate DOI/key candidates, planned-role confirmations and known consumer integrations within actual authorized scope. No universal project/mentee completeness percentage follows from a valid schema. Remember issue dispositions rather than repeatedly raising unchanged findings.

Use [check-in message and guided conversation](check-in.md) for interactive check-ins and scheduled message presentation: suggestions first, then CV/site links, then linked category checklist and a Nothing to add closeout. The user submits changes to the assistant. Respect regular-reminder versus quiet notification choices and avoid same-cycle repeated nudges.
